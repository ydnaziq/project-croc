# Art

NES-style cast + tileset. Everything is generated by `Tools/cast_gen.py`, so edits
should be made there and regenerated rather than painted over by hand — unless you
decide to take the .aseprite files as the source of truth from here on.

## Regenerating the cast

    cd Art/Tools
    python3 cast_gen.py ../ExportedSprites ../RawSprites
    aseprite --batch --script ../ExportedSprites/build.lua
    cd ../..
    for n in croc penguin cat robot slime; do
      aseprite --batch "Art/RawSprites/$n.aseprite" \
        --sheet "Art/ExportedSprites/${n}_sheet.png" --sheet-type horizontal \
        --data "Art/ExportedSprites/${n}_sheet.json" --format json-array \
        --filename-format '{tag}_{tagframe}' --list-tags
    done

`build.lua` uses paths relative to `Art/Tools`, so it must be run from there.

## Power-ups and the coin — `ExportedSprites/*.png`

Also 16x16, 5 colours, 1px black outline, generated by `Tools/food_gen.py`. They all
carry a hard white glint that none of the five foods has, because a buff that reads as
food is a bad surprise when you bite it expecting points.

| Sprite | Columns used | Palette |
|--------|--------------|---------|
| slow | 16 | `#000000` `#f8f8f8` `#383870` `#6878c8` `#f8d878` |
| shield | 14 | `#000000` `#f8f8f8` `#bcbcbc` `#7c7c7c` `#58d854` |
| magnet | 10 | `#000000` `#f83800` `#bcbcbc` `#7c7c7c` `#f8f8f8` |
| goldtooth | 8 | `#000000` `#f8f8f8` `#f8d878` `#f8b838` `#a84400` |
| coin | 16 | `#000000` `#f8f8f8` `#f8d878` `#f8b838` `#a84400` |

**The column count is not decoration.** Each sprite's drawn width matches the `width`
declared for it in `Resources/food.json`, because a food's own width *is* its timing
window - a sprite wider or narrower than its hitbox is the window lying about its size.
`food_gen.py` prints the used column count for exactly this reason, so a mismatch is
visible the moment it is regenerated.

The coin is deliberately plain in the middle: the pot's value is drawn on top of it and
has to stay legible at 8px.

`food.png` is the same five as an 80x16 strip in that order (frame `i` at
`x = i * 16`), for use as a single atlas.

## Cosmetics — `ExportedSprites/Cosmetics/*.png`

Four 16x16 accessories the croc wears, generated by `Tools/cosmetic_gen.py`. They
replaced a colour tint: multiplying a flat five-colour sprite by a colour produces
colours that are in no palette and mostly just makes the croc muddy.

| Id | Object | Anchor |
|----|--------|--------|
| `skin_chef` | a chef's toque | (0, -11) |
| `skin_gold` | one oversized gold fang | (4, 4) |
| `skin_shadow` | wraparound shades | (0, -4) |
| `skin_neon` | a small crown | (0, -12) |

The anchor is where the accessory sits on the croc's 32x32 frame, measured from its
centre. `cosmetic_gen.py` prints them on every run and `Scripts/CrocView.cs` uses those
exact numbers. The worn sprite is a **child** of the croc, so it squashes with the bite -
an accessory that holds still while the head deforms reads as a sticker on the screen.

The ids are unchanged from when these were tints, so existing saves keep what they
bought.

## The crowd

The stands are **not** in `arena.png`. `arena_gen.py` draws the empty stand at
y=140..172 and `Scripts/Crowd.cs` draws two staggered rows of silhouettes over it every
frame, at y=150 and y=162 in the same `crowd` / `crowd_l` values as the rest of the
arena. A baked crowd cannot bob, stand up or react, and a crowd that cannot do those
things is furniture.

## Animations

Each character .aseprite holds 30 frames with six tags:

| Tag | Frames | What it does |
|-----|--------|--------------|
| `idle` | 0-3 | breathing bob — the body squashes and stretches while the feet stay planted |
| `celebrate` | 4-11 | crouch, hop 2px, arms/flippers thrown out sideways, eyes squeezed shut |
| `eat` | 12-17 | lean back, mouth open wide, then two chewing frames with the mouth closed |
| `flinch` | 18-20 | knocked back two rows, eyes squeezed shut, mouth clamped - a bomb or a lost tooth |
| `gulp` | 21-24 | mouth shut, one swallow with the body bobbing - a banked pot or a buff taken |
| `taunt` | 25-29 | every tooth showing - before the bell, and when the rival is panicking |

New tags are always **appended**. Inserting one would renumber every frame after it,
and every reader of these sheets indexes by frame number.

Frame durations are baked into the .aseprite (90-180ms) and repeated in the
exported JSON, so timing comes across in any engine that reads it.

Per character the exports are:

- `<name>.png` — frame 0 only, the static portrait
- `<name>_sheet.png` — all 30 frames in a 960x32 horizontal strip, 32px cells
- `<name>_sheet.json` — Aseprite json-array data: per-frame rects, durations, and
  the tag ranges above

The eating animation deliberately contains no food — the character just opens up
and chews, so you can draw whatever it is eating separately.

## Food and hazards — `ExportedSprites/*.png`

16x16, same treatment as the cast. The five edible props come from
`Tools/food_gen.py`; the hazards and the golden bite come from `Tools/props_gen.py`.

| Sprite | Edible | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| hotdog, pizza, burger | yes | available from the first item |
| donut, pie | yes | worth more, appear after 5 eaten |
| golden | yes | rare (weight 1 against 5-6), worth 60, and only 12px wide, so its timing window is tighter than anything else's |
| bomb | no | appears after 18 eaten; chomping it costs a strike |
| boot | no | appears after 30 eaten |

Spawn odds are the `weight` column in `food.json`; a missing weight means 1.

Which items exist is `Resources/food.json`; how often they appear is
`Core/Difficulty.cs`. Adding a food is one JSON row plus one PNG.

## Arena — `ExportedSprites/arena.png`

The full 180x320 backdrop, authored by `Tools/arena_gen.py`: bunting, an EAT OFF
banner, a stage shelf, crowd silhouettes, and a plank floor. It replaced a single
brick tile stamped across the screen, which had no structure above 16px and read as
noise. Marquee bulbs are animated in code on top of it.

## Fonts — `Fonts/`

Silkscreen (SIL Open Font License, see `Fonts/OFL.txt`). A pixel font drawn on an 8px
grid, so only sizes 8, 16, and 24 are used - anything else resamples and goes soft.

## Audio — `Audio/*.ogg`

Six generated sound effects. See `Audio/README.md` for the prompts and the
regeneration steps.

## Tileset — `tileset.png`

64x80, 20 tiles of 16x16 in a 4-wide grid. Tile ids are row-major from 0:

```
 0 grass top-left    1 grass top      2 grass top-right   3 stone block
 4 dirt left edge    5 dirt fill      6 dirt right edge   7 brick
 8 platform left     9 platform mid  10 platform right   11 ladder
12 water surface    13 water fill    14 spikes           15 crate
16 coin             17 background   18 ground 1-wide    19 dirt 1-wide
```

Tiles 0-7 and 15-19 are solid; 8-10 are one-way platforms; 11 is a climbable;
12-13 are fluid; 14 is a hazard.

## Godot note

Set these textures to **Nearest** filtering (Import tab, or
`rendering/textures/canvas_textures/default_texture_filter = 0` in Project
Settings) or the pixels will come out blurry when scaled.

## Regenerating

    python3 Tools/cast_gen.py /tmp/cast     # writes the source PNGs + 8x previews
    python3 Tools/food_gen.py /tmp/food     # same, for the food props

Then re-import into Aseprite as **RGB** colour mode. Do not build these as
Indexed and then call `set_palette` — that remaps every pixel to index 0 and
silently flattens the sprite to solid black.
